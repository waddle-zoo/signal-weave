# OrbitWorks: onboarding, recurring reviews and follow-through

The current native build supported two business questions through six source
cutoffs: which renewals need review, and what drives settled cash. Both agents
delivered the two source-supported Customer Success referrals and followed the
earlier issue through its later recorded resolution. SignalWeave's agent sent
fewer unneeded status updates, but required two separately recorded resumptions.
This is functional evidence, not a clean onboarding, speed or cost win.

The [compact record](../../evaluations/data/orbitworks-whole-journey-2026-10-19-summary.json)
retains executable identities, source cutoffs, every episode's completion and
usage status, terminal hashes and actual communication counts. Large traces,
databases, credentials and working homes stay outside Git.

## The actual journey

One fictional six-account subscription company; six dependent reviews from
June 1 to July 21, 2026. Each review used a fresh Luna xhigh analyst with its
own retained workspace. Both arms had the same real read-only company MCP,
SQL/code access and independently maintained colleague conversations. The
baseline kept its own queries and notes; it was not forced to forget them.
Treatment additionally used the native executable, bundled SignalWeave skill
and live Jev. Simulated colleagues used Sol low. Neither arm received a
finished analytical query, card or answer key.

Treatment's checksum-verified **locally built** archive was installed into a
fresh private home. The actual PTY wizard stored the key, connected the supplied
IT-reviewed MCP bridge, registered the executable with isolated Codex and
installed its skill. This was scripted operator input, not novice-human reading
time or a test of the public download endpoint. The connection was a company
SQLite MCP, not Superset or Preset; arbitrary MCP normalization was not automatic.

The analyst discovered the data, asked the owner, wrote its query and drafted
its card. The owner rejected a latest-calendar-month cash scope and required an
inclusive trailing 30×24-hour window. The agent revised it. The approved v5
preserved that definition, renewal triggers, source scope and batch-coverage
limits. The baseline obtained its cash-window clarification later and corrected
its own SQL and messages. That earlier ambiguity is not a violation of a rule
the baseline had never been told.

## What reached people

| Work | BI tools + Luna | SignalWeave-assisted agent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Observed qualifying July 11 renewal | Harborlight: failed payment + open billing case; invoice not yet overdue | Same supported review reason |
| Observed qualifying July 21 renewal | Talus: $750 invoice past due | Same supported review reason |
| Prior Harborlight follow-up | Later source-recorded paid invoice, completed renewal and resolved case | Same; no invented cause or CS action |
| Final trailing-window USD cash | $14,400 charges − $300 refunds = $14,100 visible net | Same; no unsupported FX conversion or completeness claim |
| No-trigger Customer Success FYIs, first four cutoffs | Five, including a corrected version | Zero |
| Delivered local messages, all stages | 26 | 15 |

These are observed messages, not an alert-precision percentage or proof of
hours saved. Across all roles, there were 27 simulated colleague replies in
baseline and 23 in treatment; these are not counts of owner interruptions or
card reviews. Extra consent requests were separate process burden.
Treatment's three recorded
report/source attachment opens were all in the first Data Operations exchange;
six additional opens were card-consent artifacts. Baseline recorded no
attachment opens. Correct inline messages—not copied files alone—support the
usefulness finding.

The agent, not SignalWeave's delivery subsystem, sent the local inbox messages.
Jev supplied narrow saved-policy judgments; the frontier agent did SQL,
arithmetic, investigation and the final explanation. Source-recorded resolution
was not treated as proof that a colleague caused or completed a repair.

## Failures and remaining friction

Original treatment onboarding hit its 900-second response limit. A separate
235-second continuation completed its remaining review work. July 11 later hit
the 360-second limit after delivering the useful handoffs; a separate
51-second administrative recovery closed that session with **no new queries,
Jev calls or duplicate messages**. Both original failures and their unknown
outer model usage remain in the record. The final July 21 reviews completed.

The frozen study instructions also induced redundant owner consent on unchanged
recurring cards. The product already documents direct approved-card reuse;
that instruction was corrected prospectively for the final closeout, not
retroactively rescored. Even that final treatment review took 291 seconds
versus baseline's 206. Fast policy calls did not make the whole job faster.

Human-facing treatment briefs were still wordy and sometimes included internal
delivery/scheduling mechanics. One compact evaluation returned about 50 KB;
fewer direct source calls did not eliminate frontier-context overhead. These
are concrete simplification targets, not reasons to invent another orchestration
layer.

Internal data and product reviewers checked actual queries, source facts,
messages, recipient access and feedback. Their review also caught a test-design
gap: all six dates happen to give equal monthly and rolling-window cash totals.
Correct period definitions were reviewed, but numeric window divergence was
not exercised. This is one known fictional company—not enterprise-scale,
external peer review, a real-user study, hosted multi-user validation, real
Slack delivery or evidence that Jev alone caused the communication difference.
