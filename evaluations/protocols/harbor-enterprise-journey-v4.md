## What changed and why

- The source's `tickets` inspection now exposes a versioned ordinary field dictionary. It documents this company's binary priority-work flag, reconstructed status and visible-response null semantics. Both arms obtain it through the same source tool, not private hints. It contains no counts, threshold, expected decision or future row. This documentation was absent in v3; v3 is not rescored under it.
- Exact saved native cards larger than the simulated person's inline-message limit are delivered as owned, hash-tracked review attachments rather than truncated or rejected. Record actual reads and replies. No reply automatically approves a native card, and approval is never inferred from a canned acknowledgment.
- The owner can explicitly authorize the proposed local review-draft delivery within the agreed policy. Authorization is different from executing a send. Card approval alone is not delivery permission. External operational sends, assignments and repairs remain out of scope. No reader is forced to complain or request changes to a good answer.
- Generic bundled agent guidance distinguishes source/data-expert technical meanings from business-owner policy. Missing documentation is not a proven negative mapping. Unresolved assumptions stay open questions rather than established definitions. This is not a hidden-answer prompt or permission to let Jev do analysis.
- The final workspace observer records bounded hashes and unfollowed symlinks; it cannot turn a finished analyst episode into a missing cost record. Its coverage is not a business grade.
